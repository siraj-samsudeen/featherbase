Shell And Page

Every page is one white panel on the grey canvas. The sidebar is the platform's: show the placeholder, never a sidebar of your own. The panel fills the space after it, with no width cap, and spaces the blocks inside it evenly. In the app the panel scrolls and the sidebar stays; add ?full to the address when you need a screenshot of the whole page.

Headline

      
The answer-first block that leads a report page: the period, the conclusion in words, the supporting figures, and optionally progress toward a target. One per page. Tones: --negative, --caution, --positive, --missing; with no tone class it is neutral. For a figure against a target: below 90% is negative, 90% up to 100% caution, 100% and over positive, and no target is missing. The meter's scale is 0 to 100, or to the figure when it is over 100; the line marks a level on that scale.

Card

      
One group of related content inside the page: a quiet grey block, told apart by its fill, never a line or a shadow. Do not put a whole list or table screen in a card. fb-raised is the other surface: something that floats over the page (a menu, a popover).

Button

      
A button is a fill, never a box. --primary for the one main action of a page or form, plain for the others, --quiet for low-weight actions inside cards, --danger for deleting or discarding. A button whose menu is open carries aria-expanded="true". A link that must read as a button takes the same classes.

Segmented

      
Two to five choices that change the view in place (a toggle row). The chosen one carries aria-pressed="true". With featherbase-look.js on the page a click moves the choice and the group fires fb:change. For sections of a page use Content Tabs; for more choices, a select. Toggle Row, below, shows two of them with their labels.

Status Pill

      
A short status in words; the colour only repeats what the words say. Tones: --negative (below target, errors), --caution (near target), --positive (met), --opportunity, --missing (no data). With no tone class it is the neutral blue, for a state that is neither good nor bad ("In progress", "Changed"). It never wraps, so keep the words short.

Chip And Tag

      
A chip is a tinted pill with a meaning. A status is green when live, amber when not yet and grey when finished, with a dot. A category takes one of eight tints (blue, teal, violet, pink, orange, lime, cyan, yellow) and keeps it on every page. Red is kept for a data problem. A tag (fb-tag) is the neutral one, for a source, a reach or a level.

A select is a real <select> inside fb-select; a search box sits inside fb-search.

Record Link

      
Every Featherbase record shown on any screen (a person, a store, a section, a department, a ticket) is a link that opens it: its readable name, then who or what it is. Name the record with data-table and data-row so the builder knows where the link goes. The one plain form, fb-record-link--text, is for a record the viewer may not open.

Person Label

      
A person is always named so they can be told apart: the name as a link to the Person, then employee code · role · place. Stacked (the words on a second line) for headers, pickers and search; inline for table cells and activity lines. A person with more than one current code shows the main one and a "+1 code" mark. Inside a sentence use the inline link without the fb-person wrapper, so it wraps like the words around it.

Layout And Text Helpers

      
Spacing and text classes, so a prototype needs little CSS of its own. Layout: fb-stack (one under the other, 16 px apart; --tight 8, --loose 24), fb-row (side by side, 8 px apart, wrapping; --loose 16, --between pushes the ends apart), fb-grid (equal columns that wrap; --2 for exactly two), fb-push (to the right end of a row). Text: fb-section-title, fb-text (14 px body), fb-small (12 px), fb-label-text (a 13 px label), fb-muted and fb-subtle (quieter), fb-strong, fb-num (a number: right-aligned, figures of one width), and the meaning colours fb-positive, fb-caution, fb-negative. fb-link is a plain link that is not a record.

Icons

      
The icons the components use, drawn by a class: fb-icon fb-icon--<name>. An icon takes the colour of its text and is 18 px; fb-icon--14, --16, --20 and --28 size it. Do not draw or paste another icon.

Pattern: A Table With Grouped Column Bands not yet a shared component

      
A first header row names each group of columns. Groups are set apart by a gap of the page's own colour, never a line: put fb-band-start on the first cell of each group in every row. A column outside every group gets fb-band-none in both header rows; when it comes after the groups, give its cells fb-band-start too, so the gap closes the last group.

Pattern: "Built From": A Share Bar And A Donut partly a shared component: PartsBar

      
What a figure is made of. Each part takes a series colour (fb-series-1 to fb-series-5; these never mean good or bad) and its share as --share. The bar is for a table cell or a narrow space; the donut for a block with room. The donut's ring is the --fb-donut value that fbLook.donut([46, 31, 23]) returns. Always give the legend: colour alone is never the only signal. The small bar (fb-sharebar--small) and the small donut (fb-donut--small) are the shared PartsBar component, for a table cell; the large donut with its legend is not a component yet. The parts of a forecast have colours of their own, the same on every screen: fb-part-recent, fb-part-step, fb-part-last-year and fb-part-adjust, used in place of a series colour.
