Glossary wording for Dash, Tasker and Tickets

75 drafted terms, 3 apps. Each is drawn from the app's own specs or code, and each card names its source. Nothing is committed to the apps' final wording until you approve it. Words the instance already defines (its 40 terms) are left out, so every app's glossary shows its own terms plus those 40.

Questions for youDash (28)Tasker (27)Tickets (20)
Questions for you (17). These words are used by the apps but no source defines them, so I did not invent a meaning.
  • Dash Buyer Section: shown on screens, moves go to the master data team, but nothing defines it. What is it?
  • Dash Merchandise Assignment and Category head: visible only in code. Do you want them in the glossary, and with what meaning?
  • Dash Area (Sales, Inventory, Buying, Operations, HR, Finance) on the Report access page: how should it be explained?
  • Dash Cached: how often is the stored copy refreshed?
  • Dash Unnamed subcategory: why does it happen and what should the reader do?
  • Dash Dash Reader: left out. Its meaning is only read from its grants, not stated anywhere. Please give the wording.
  • Tasker Director: who is the director, and what does the role mean?
  • Tasker Owner function (Store operations, Facilities, IT, Finance, Supply chain, HR), issue category and issue severity: please define them.
  • Tasker Containment ("Done right away"): what counts?
  • Tasker Evidence photo: when is it required?
  • Tasker Blocked versus On hold: what is the difference?
  • Tasker Tasker's own Store list (a place of work marked Store or Warehouse, with a manager and opening times) is not the instance's Store. Is that intended? Both will show side by side.
  • Tickets Which word for the person who raised a ticket: "Requester" (the column label) or "Raiser" (the specs)? The glossary says Raiser for now.
  • Tickets Which word for support's updates: "Public progress" or "Public update"? The glossary says Public progress for now.
  • Tickets Data Quality Issue: used as a ticket type but never defined. How does it relate to the instance's "Data quality ticket"?
  • Tickets Routing, Evidence / Link work, Wording, and the built-in states Needs verification, Awaiting approval and Policy review: not defined anywhere. Wording needed.
  • Tickets "State" is renamed "Ticket state", because the instance already has a term "State" (an Indian state, with its GST code).

Gap

Worked out from the code

Sales minus target for a subcategory, section or the whole table. A negative gap (shown in red) means sales are below target. It is shown only where both a target and sales were recorded.

Examples
  • Sales 90 against target 100 gives a gap of minus 10.

Source: client-apps/jeyarama/dash/src/report.ts totalOf (gap = actual - target, rounded to 2 places); src/app.tsx RowFigures (negative styled, shown only when both recorded)

Achieved

Worked out from the code

Sales as a percentage of target. It is left blank when there is no target to measure against.

Examples

Source: client-apps/jeyarama/dash/src/report.ts totalOf (achievement = 100 * actual / target, null when target is 0 or missing); src/app.tsx column header "Achieved"

Tasker 27 terms

Tasks, projects, issues and their verification.

Inbox

Stated in the app

Where the tasks you capture privately wait until you organise them. It holds only your own captures; you can keep a task on your personal list or move it into a shared project.

Examples
  • Typing a title and pressing Enter captures a task that only you can see in your Inbox.
Describes

tasker.task

Source: openspec/specs/tasker-process-inbox-items/spec.md (What the Inbox holds); tasker-quick-task-capture/spec.md

Personal task

Stated in the app

A task that only its creator can see or change, with its subtasks, discussion and history. Even an administrator cannot open it. Nobody else can be made responsible for it until it is shared.

Describes

tasker.task.personal_tasks_owner

Source: openspec/specs/tasker-shared-team-work/spec.md (Personal work is creator-only); tasker-process-inbox-items/spec.md

Sharing a task

Stated in the app

Moving a personal task into a shared project shares it, with its description, subtasks, discussion and history, immediately and without a confirmation. It can never be made private again.

Examples
  • Moving a private task into Warehouse review makes it visible to members; moving it back is refused.
Describes

tasker.task.shared

Source: openspec/specs/tasker-shared-team-work/spec.md (Sharing is deliberate and permanent)

Responsible person

Stated in the app

The one teammate who has taken a task on. A task has at most one, and anyone can assign, reassign or take it. It is separate from progress and urgency, so changing one does not change the others.

Words to avoid
  • Owner: Tasks use responsible person (assigned to); owner is the word used for an issue.
Describes

tasker.task.assigned_to

Source: openspec/specs/tasker-responsibility-and-progress/spec.md (One responsible person)

Task state

Stated in the app

How far along a task is: Not started, In progress, Blocked, On hold, Submitted for verification, Done or Cancelled. Ticking a task marks it Done and unticking restores the state it had just before; only Done shows as ticked.

Describes

tasker.task.task_state

Source: openspec/specs/tasker-responsibility-and-progress/spec.md (Ticking done can be undone); example-apps/tasker/featherbase.json task_state choices

Urgent

Stated in the app

One flag, shared by the whole team, that marks a task as urgent or not. Changing it does not change who is responsible or anyone's My Focus.

tasker.task.urgent

Stated in the app

Your own private, ordered shortlist of tasks, which can include tasks you are not responsible for. Starring a task into it changes nothing for anyone else.

Stated in the app

Recurring task

Stated in the app

A task that repeats on a daily, weekly or monthly schedule, or a set interval after completion. Only one occurrence is unfinished at a time and missed slots are skipped, not piled up. Each new occurrence has fresh unchecked subtasks and does not copy the old discussion. Cancelling the active occurrence pauses the series until a member resumes it.

Examples

Ticket state

Stated in the app

Where a ticket is in its life, shown on the queue and the ticket. A new ticket starts in the first state its type lists; a ticket in a done state counts as resolved and leaves the open queue.

Examples
  • Built-in states: New, Needs verification, Awaiting approval, Approved, Rejected, Policy review, In progress, Resolved.

Transition

Stated in the app

An action a ticket type lists that moves a ticket from one state to another. It names who may take it (the assignee, the raiser, anyone involved, or holders of a role) and what they must write down. When a type lists transitions, the server refuses every other change of state.

Examples
  • Bala confirms a raised issue with what will be fixed, where, and a target date; the ticket moves to 'Fix planned'.

Private request

Stated in the app

The original message and structured details of a ticket whose type keeps its details private. Only the raiser, support and the people the ticket names (its assignee and the person it is raised for) read it. All built-in types are private.

Stated in the app

One entry in the history of a private ticket: who did what and when, such as an assignment, a transition, a change of raiser or a removed rule. On a private ticket only support reads the full history; everyone else sees only which actions were taken.

Data quality rule

Stated in the app

A filter on one linked Table, kept on a ticket, that describes a gap in master data, such as every buyer whose person is blank. It has a severity (High, Medium or Low), an owner and how to fix it, and shows a live count of rows still to fix, or all clear when none are. A rule is removed only with a reason, and changed by removing it and adding a new one.

Examples
  • 'Buyer is blank' on Buyer Assignment: 3 of 3 rows left, then 2 of 3 as people are filled in.
